Laboratory Protocol

Push-out Bond Strength Between Ti-Base and Zirconia Using Varied Bonding Protocols

Step-by-step cementation guide for all experimental groups. All specimens are self-cured with no light activation to replicate the clinical condition and standardize curing across groups.

Specimens & Components

Materials Overview

Specimens were milled from pre-sintered high-translucency 4Y-TZP discs (KATANA Zirconia HTML PLUS, shade A2; Lot# EPQNZ and EPWWX, Kuraray Noritake Dental Inc., Tokyo, Japan) with a 5-axis milling unit (Model 580DC; XTCERA, Shenzhen, China), incorporating a 20 µm overmill to facilitate cement space and adaptation. Specimens were sintered in a high-temperature furnace (HTS-2/M/Zirkon-120; MIHM-VOGT GmbH & Co. KG, Stutensee, Germany) using the manufacturer’s standard sintering program: heating rate of 10 °C/min to 1550 °C, 2-hour hold at peak temperature, followed by controlled furnace cooling with removal at 800 °C (total cycle approximately 7 hours). These are the same discs used in the connector study (Ramos, Pacheco et al., submitted to JPD).

Before You Begin

Common Surface Preparation

Perform these steps for every specimen in every group before moving to the group-specific protocol.

Surface Preparation (All Groups) Universal
  1. Air-abrade the intaglio (bonding) surface of each zirconia specimen with 50 µm aluminum oxide at approximately 2 bar, 10 mm distance, for 15 seconds.
  2. Ultrasonically clean in isopropanol for 3 minutes (or steam clean). Dry completely with oil-free compressed air.
  3. Clean the bonding surface of each Ti-base with isopropanol on gauze or microbrush. Dry completely.
  4. From this point forward, handle specimens only with clean cotton pliers or clean gloves. Do not touch bonding surfaces with bare fingers or contaminated instruments.
Contamination Warning

If a primed or cleaned surface is contaminated at any point (moisture, saliva, powder residue from gloves, accidental touch), re-clean the surface and re-prime from the beginning. Do not proceed with a contaminated surface.

Experimental Groups

Group-Specific Bonding Protocols

Each group follows its own primer and cement combination. All groups are cured by chemical (self-cure) mechanism only, with no light activation.

Group 1: MBML (n=10) Ivoclar
Materials

Primer: Monobond Plus (Ivoclar Vivadent AG, Schaan, Liechtenstein)

REF# 626221AN  |  Lot# Z05GC4

Cement: Multilink Automix (Ivoclar Vivadent AG, Schaan, Liechtenstein)

REF# 615218WW  |  Lot# Z05HYT
Priming: Both Surfaces
  1. Apply a thin layer of Monobond Plus to the intaglio surface of the zirconia with a clean microbrush.
  2. Apply Monobond Plus to the bonding surface of the Ti-base with a separate clean microbrush.
  3. Allow both surfaces to react for 60 seconds.
  4. Disperse excess with a strong stream of oil-free air on both surfaces. Each surface should show a thin, uniform film.
Cementation
  1. Dispense Multilink Automix from the automix syringe. Discard the first small extrusion to ensure proper mix ratio.
  2. Apply cement to the intaglio surface of the zirconia.
  3. Seat the Ti-base into the zirconia with firm, steady finger pressure.
  4. Remove gross excess with a microbrush before gel phase. After initial set, carefully inspect the margins and remove all residual cement flush with the zirconia/Ti-base junction using a scaler or blade. No cement should extend beyond the margin or remain on the external surface of the Ti-base, as any residual material will interfere with the push-out fixture and compromise the test.
  5. Allow to self-cure undisturbed. Do not light-cure. Minimum 5 minutes from start of mix.
  6. Record the time of cementation and specimen ID.
Self-cure · min 5 min · no light

Why Chemical Cure Only

Multilink Hybrid Abutment is designed exclusively for chemical polymerization. Ivoclar engineered this product for the Ti-base/zirconia interface where light access is impossible in clinical use. All other groups also use self-cure mode to standardize the curing protocol across the study.

After Bonding

Post-Cementation & Testing

These steps apply to all bonded specimens regardless of group.

Storage & Push-out Testing All Groups
  1. After full cure, verify that all excess cement has been completely removed from each specimen. Inspect under magnification if needed. The push-out plunger must contact only the Ti-base; any residual cement at the margin will bear against the fixture, artificially increase the recorded load, and invalidate the measurement.
  2. Store all bonded specimens in distilled water at 37 °C for 72 hours before testing (bond Friday, test Monday). Ensure all 40 specimens are bonded on the same day and tested on the same day for consistency.
  3. Mount each specimen in the custom stainless steel push-out fixture.
  4. Test on the Instron 5544 at a crosshead speed of 1.0 mm/min until failure.
  5. Record maximum load at failure in Newtons (N).
  6. Calculate push-out bond strength using the predefined formula.
  7. Examine debonded surfaces under the Olympus optical microscope.
  8. Classify failure mode: adhesive, cohesive in Ti-base, cohesive in zirconia, or mixed.
  9. Select representative specimens for SEM analysis (collaboration with Federal University of Uberlandia).
Important Reminders

General Notes

Critical: Cement Removal for Push-out Testing

This is a push-out bond strength test. The plunger in the custom stainless steel fixture applies compressive force directly to the Ti-base to dislodge it from the zirconia. If any cured cement remains on the external surface of the Ti-base or extends beyond the zirconia/Ti-base margin, it will bear against the fixture wall during loading. This creates a false resistance that inflates the recorded failure load and does not represent the actual bond strength at the cementation interface. After bonding, carefully inspect each specimen and remove every trace of excess cement from the margin and external Ti-base surfaces using a scaler, blade, or finishing bur under magnification. The bonding interface should be the only connection between the two components.

Seating Pressure

Maintain a consistent seating load across all specimens. If a custom jig or standardized weight is available, use it. If using finger pressure, keep it as uniform as possible across operators and specimens.

Labeling

Label each specimen clearly with group abbreviation and specimen number (e.g., MBML-01, MZGC-05) before bonding. Photograph relevant steps for the methods write-up if needed.

Automix Syringe Tip

Always discard the first small extrusion from each automix syringe to ensure the base and catalyst are mixing at the correct ratio. Replace the mixing tip if more than 10 minutes have passed since the last extrusion.

Statistical Analysis

Data will be analyzed using one-way ANOVA and Tukey multiple comparison test (alpha = 0.05) in GraphPad Prism 10.

Storage Timing (72 Hours)

Bond all 40 specimens on Friday. Store in distilled water at 37 °C. Test all on Monday (~72 hours). The 72-hour storage ensures full polymerization of all cements, particularly the chemical-cure-only Multilink Hybrid Abutment, which may not reach maximum degree of conversion by 24 hours. This eliminates incomplete curing as a confounding variable. In the methods, report: “Specimens were stored in distilled water at 37 °C for 72 hours prior to testing.”
